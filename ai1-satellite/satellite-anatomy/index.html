Anatomy of SpaceX's AI1 orbital data center

A rack of 72 AI chips flying 600 km up, powered by 70 metres of solar wings and cooled by a radiator that sheds heat straight into space. Turn it, open the compute module, and follow the power, heat and data through it.

What is public and what is drawn in: SpaceX has given AI1's outline (about 70 m wingspan, 20 m deployed height, 150 kW peak and 120 kW sustained compute, a 110 m² deployable liquid radiator with redundant pumping loops held knife-edge to the Sun, laser links instead of antennas, Starlink V3 heritage) and named NVIDIA Rubin GPUs and Vera CPUs as the first compute payload. It has not published the inside of the compute module. That interior follows NVIDIA's NVL72 rack (18 compute trays of 4 GPUs and 2 CPUs, 9 NVLink switch trays); the pumps, power shelf, battery and thrusters are typical choices, drawn as an informed illustration rather than SpaceX's own layout. AI1 is a draft design, with two prototypes planned for 2027.

Numbers on the page come from one model: 250 W/m² of solar array, about 1,400 W per m² of radiator panel (both faces radiating, emissivity 0.9), and every watt of compute ending up as heat. Sources: DCD, Tech Times, Engadget, Dealroom.
